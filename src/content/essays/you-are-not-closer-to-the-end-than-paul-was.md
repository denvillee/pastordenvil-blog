<p>A woman stopped me in the lobby after a service a few years ago and asked if I thought we were in the last days. She was not being dramatic. She had been reading the news the way a lot of us read the news, with a knot in her stomach, and she wanted to know whether the knot meant something.</p>

<p>I gave her the answer I had been trained to give, which was a careful one, and it was not wrong. But I have thought about that conversation for years, because the honest answer is stranger and better than the one I gave her.</p>

<p>Yes, we are in the last days. So was she. So was her grandmother. So was Paul.</p>

<h2>The end is not on the calendar</h2>

<p>The New Testament almost never uses the language of the last days the way we use it. We use it to mean a period still out ahead of us, getting closer, with signs along the way like mile markers. Peter stands up at Pentecost, looks at a crowd of confused people in the middle of an ordinary morning, and says this is that. Joel's last days. Now. Today. In a city, on a Tuesday, before anything had visibly ended.</p>

<blockquote>In the last days, God says, I will pour out my Spirit on all people.<cite>Acts 2:17</cite></blockquote>

<p>Which means the last days are not ahead of us. They started, and we are standing in them, and we have been standing in them for two thousand years. That is a strange sentence and I want to slow down on it, because it changes what you do on Monday.</p>

<div class="pull">Nobody alive is closer to the end than Paul was, because the end is not a date on the calendar. The end is a person, and Paul was already in him.</div>

<p>The South African theologian Adrio König made this argument in a book with a title that says the whole thing: <em>The Eclipse of Christ in Eschatology</em>. His point is that when we turn the doctrine of last things into a chapter about the future, we lose Christ inside our own timeline. We start talking about the end as a scheduled event and we stop talking about him as the one who is himself the end, the last thing, the goal that everything was always moving toward.</p>

<p>So the question is not how close we are. The question is who we are in.</p>

<h2>What that does to a Tuesday</h2>

<p>Here is where this stops being a seminary conversation.</p>

<p>If the end is a date, then waiting is passive. You watch the news, you count the signs, you hold your breath, and you hope the schedule speeds up. Your life becomes a waiting room, and waiting rooms make people either anxious or numb. I have pastored both kinds of people. I have been both kinds of people.</p>

<p>If the end is a person, then waiting is a relationship. You are not standing outside the thing you are waiting for. You are already inside it, and the question shifts from when will this arrive to what is he doing in me right now.</p>

<p>König walks it out in three movements, and they are worth memorizing because they cover almost everything you will face. Christ <strong>for us</strong>, which is the cross, God acting on behalf of people who could not act for themselves. Christ <strong>in us</strong>, which is the Spirit, the same Jesus at work inside a person who is still very much a work in progress. Christ <strong>with us</strong>, which is his coming, when presence stops being partial.</p>

<p>Most weeks you need one of those three more than the other two. The week you fail badly, you need Christ for you, and no amount of trying harder will substitute. The week you are stuck in the same argument with the same person for the fourth time, you need Christ in you, forming something slower than you would like. The week you sit in a hospital room, you need the promise that he will be with us, and that it will not always be like this.</p>

<h2>The overlap you actually live in</h2>

<p>The old picture of two ages is still useful. This age, the one with hospitals and traffic and funerals, is still running. The age to come broke in early at the resurrection and has not finished arriving. We live in the overlap, which is why the church is never as healed, as unified, or as finished as we want it to be, and also why anything gets healed at all.</p>

<p>But put Christ underneath that picture and it stops being a timeline you are located on. It becomes a person you are located in.</p>

<p>I wish I had said that to her in the lobby. We are in the last days. We have been the whole time. And the thing you are waiting for is already holding you.</p>
