Think about the last time you met a new doctor.

Before you ever shook hands, somebody handed them a chart. Your history, your symptoms, your medications, your problems.... they knew what was wrong with you before they knew you.

And to be fair, that's their job. You came in because something hurt. But have you ever noticed how strange it feels when the first questions are all about what's broken?

You want to say, *I'm more than this chart. I had a life before this diagnosis. Ask me who I am.*

Now be honest.... isn't that sometimes how we've learned to introduce people to God?

We often start the good news at the bad news.

Humans sinned. Humans are separated from God. Humans face judgment. Jesus died so we can be saved.

Every sentence in that summary touches something true.

But listen to what it does.

It hands God our chart before it tells him our name.

It defines the person by the problem.

**The Bible doesn't start there. And I've come to believe that where you start changes everything.**

## The order of the story

Before Scripture tells us what went wrong with humanity, it tells us who we are.

Genesis 1 comes before Genesis 3.

That sounds obvious, but sit with it.

Sometimes we've learned to tell the gospel as though the story begins a few pages later.

In the actual story, God creates and calls it good. God makes human beings in his image, male and female. God blesses them.... blesses them!.... and gives them a world, a purpose, and his own presence.

Goodness.

Identity.

Intention.

All of it comes first.

Then, and only then, something goes terribly wrong.

Sin is real, and the Bible never whispers about it. But notice what sin explains and what it doesn't.

Sin explains what has happened to human beings.

It does not provide the original definition of human beings.

You were made in the image of God.... not in the image of sin.

Sin is terrible precisely because something good has been fractured.

If nothing glorious was there first, the Fall wouldn't be a tragedy. It would just be a fact.

To put it another way:

> [!BIG]
> Identity precedes activity.

Who God made us to be is older than what we've done, for better and for worse.

## Conviction and conversation

Now, somebody's going to push back, and the pushback deserves a fair hearing.

Don't people need to know they're sinners?

Doesn't the good news require the bad news?

Yes.

Here's the distinction that's helped me.

There's a difference between where a conversation starts and where a conviction starts.

When I sit with someone, we may well begin with their felt brokenness.... the guilt, the grief, the addiction, the fear. That's pastoral wisdom. You start where the person is.

But underneath that conversation, my conviction about the person can't begin in Genesis 3.

It has to begin in Genesis 1.

Because the person in front of me is not fundamentally a problem to be solved.

They're someone God made.

Someone bearing his image.

Someone whose life has intention behind it, made for relationship and purpose and the presence of God.

The chart is real....

and the chart is not the person.

And this changes how salvation itself sounds.

If my anthropology begins in Genesis 3, salvation can easily become mostly rescue from what's wrong with us. An escape hatch with a cross on the door.

But if it begins in Genesis 1, salvation becomes God's restoration of what he intended for us all along.

> [!BIG]
> You cannot understand salvation until you understand what God is saving.

Some gospel presentations ask:

*What are you being saved <span class="c">from</span>?*

The Bible keeps pressing another question:

*What are you being saved <span class="c">for</span>?*

Forgiveness still matters, but now forgiveness belongs inside restoration.

Reconciliation brings us back into relationship.

The Spirit makes God's presence real among us.

Discipleship begins restoring vocation.

The church becomes a people learning again how to belong to God and to one another.

Resurrection restores embodied life.

New creation restores the world human beings were always meant to share with God.

That's not a different gospel.

It's the same gospel with the whole story attached.

Jesus didn't come simply to get you somewhere better when you die.

God has acted in Jesus to reclaim what sin ruined and begin making all things new.

Turn toward the King and enter the life you were made for.

## Where does your gospel begin?

So here's the question I'd send you home with, whether you're a pastor, a parent, a small group leader, or the friend somebody calls at midnight.

When you think about the person in front of you.... your prodigal kid, your difficult coworker, the stranger who has no idea what you believe.... where does your gospel begin?

Is the first thing you see a sinner who needs rescue?

Or an image-bearer whose sin has fractured something God intended for good?

Both matter.

But one tells you where the story begins.

And I think you'll talk differently depending on your answer.

You'll listen differently.

You may even love differently.

Because the Christian story is meant to be carried by a vision of what God intends to restore, not merely by fear of what happens if we refuse him.

Before you ask what's wrong with us, ask who we are.

God did.
