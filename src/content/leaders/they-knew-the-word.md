There is a sentence in my notes that I keep coming back to, and it is not
comfortable.

> The Pharisees understood love based on a book they read, memorized, and
> recited. They knew the word. But when the word became flesh, they didn't
> recognize it.

These were not careless people. They were the most careful people in the room.
They tithed their spices. Mint, dill and cumin, weighed out. That is not
hypocrisy in the lazy sense; that is enormous effort, aimed with total sincerity
at the wrong target.

Jesus names the miss precisely:

> You have neglected the more important matters of the law: justice, mercy and
> faithfulness.

And in John:

> You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal
> life; and it is they that bear witness about me, yet you refuse to come to me
> that you may have life.

You can search the thing that points at him and use the searching as a reason
not to go where it points.

## Why this is a leadership problem and not a Pharisee problem

Because handling the text is our job.

If you preach, teach, lead a group, write the study, or answer the questions,
then knowing the word is your competence. It is what people call you for. And
competence is exactly the thing that can stand in for recognition, because it
feels so much like it. You can be the most biblically literate person in the
building and be the last one to notice what God is actually doing in it.

Jeremiah had already drawn the line:

> Let not the wise boast of their wisdom or the strong boast of their strength
> or the rich boast of their riches, but let the one who boasts boast about
> this: that they have the understanding to know me.

Wisdom, strength, riches. Those are the three things leaders accumulate. Jeremiah
does not say they are worthless. He says they are the wrong thing to boast in,
and boasting is just the visible form of what you are quietly resting on.

It is not enough to know about God.

## What changes it

Not more study. I am not going to tell a room of leaders to read less.

What changes it is being on the receiving end of something.

> The more I allow God to love me the more he makes sense to me.

Notice the order. Not: the more I understand him, the more I can receive from
him. The other way round. Understanding arrives behind experience, and knowing
that God is loving doesn't benefit us unless we experience it.

And there is a cost to skipping that, which lands on the people you lead:

> Whatever kind of love we believe God withholds we will also withhold.

That is the whole thing, really. A leader who believes God is holding out on him
will hold out on his people, and will have Scripture to justify every inch of
it. The Pharisees did. They had chapter and verse for the distance they kept.

## Live in the world. Live in the word.

Both. That is the line I wrote and I am keeping it.

Not the word instead of the world, which produces people who are fluent and
unrecognizable. Not the world instead of the word, which produces people with
nothing to say. Both, at the same time, on purpose.

The word became flesh and moved into the neighborhood. The people who had
memorized the book did not recognize him, because they were expecting a text and
he showed up as a person.

He still does that.
